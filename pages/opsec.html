OpSec & Security Awareness Training

Training that helps teams spot scams, protect company information and respond fast if something goes wrong.

Phishing & Deceptive Links

  • URL distinction: Spotting lookalike domains (paypa1.com, micros0ft.com), misspellings, odd subdomains (paypal.com.secure-login.net) and shortened links. It also covers the fact that the padlock/HTTPS does not mean a site is safe.
  • Phishing email recognition: Fake senders, urgent or threatening language, unexpected attachments and requests to "verify your account."
  • Smishing: Phishing through text messages, like fake package delivery or bank alerts.
  • Quishing: Malicious QR codes on flyers, parking meters or in emails.

Impersonation & Social Engineering

  • Caller ID spoof awareness: Caller ID can be faked, so a call that looks like it comes from the bank, IT or the boss needs to be verified by calling back on a known number. This scam is called vishing (voice phishing).
  • Business Email Compromise: Fake emails from "the CEO" or "a vendor" asking for wire transfers, changed bank details or gift cards.
  • Pretexting: Attackers pose as IT support, delivery drivers, inspectors or new employees to gain trust or access.
  • Deepfake voice and video: AI-cloned voices of executives or family members asking for money or information.
  • MFA fatigue: Attackers spam login approval prompts hoping someone taps "Approve" just to make them stop.

Information Protection

  • Sharing sensitive company details: What's safe to discuss publicly, with vendors or over the phone and what isn't.
  • Disposal of sensitive documentation: Shredding paper, plus digital disposal: properly wiping or destroying old hard drives, phones, USB drives and printers, which store copies of documents.
  • Social media oversharing: Posts that reveal internal job titles, org charts, travel plans, office layouts or badges. Attackers use this for research (OSINT).
  • Photos and screen sharing: Whiteboards, sticky notes with passwords or open screens visible in photos and video calls.
  • AI tool data leaks: Pasting client data, passwords or internal documents into public AI chatbots.
  • Data classification: Knowing what counts as public, internal, confidential or restricted.

Passwords & Accounts

  • Strong passwords and passphrases: Never reusing them.
  • Password managers: 1Password, Bitwarden, Keeper and a few others.
  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA/2FA): Why app-based codes or hardware keys beat SMS codes (SIM swap).

Physical Security

  • Tailgating/piggybacking: Holding the door for someone who hasn't badged in.
  • Clean desk policy: No sensitive papers or passwords left out.
  • Screen locking: Locking the computer every time it's left unattended.
  • Shoulder surfing: People watching a screen or keypad in public places.
  • Unknown USB drives: Never plugging in found or unsolicited devices.
  • Visitor procedures: Sign-ins, escorts and questioning unfamiliar people.

Devices & Remote Work

  • Exposed USB ports: Securing computers in public or shared areas.
  • Personal laptops: Keeping them separate from work laptops.
  • Public Wi-Fi risks: Always using a VPN on public networks.
  • Software updates: Keeping computers, phones and routers patched with the latest security updates.
  • Home office security: Securing the home router and separating work and personal devices.
  • Lost or stolen devices: Reporting immediately so they can be locked or wiped.
  • Travel security: Hotel Wi-Fi, leaving laptops in rooms and border device searches.

Response & Culture

  • Incident reporting: What to do right after clicking a bad link or giving out info. Report fast, with no blame, because speed matters more than embarrassment.
  • Ransomware awareness: Warning signs and why backups matter.
  • Verification habits: Build a "trust but verify" culture for any unusual request, especially one involving money or credentials.

Training Delivery & Outcome

  • Snacks and beverages will be provided for on-site sessions.
  • Instructor introduction.
  • Full overview given before starting the training.
  • Follow up with a game for entertainment and engagement.
  • Crash-course style training.
  • Quick quiz on the most important topics, with easy multiple-choice answers to help with retention.

  • Ending with a Q&A with the ability to follow up directly with the instructor for more in-depth explanations.

Session Details

  • Length: 45 to 60 minutes.
  • On-site: up to 20 employees.
  • Remote: up to 10 employees.

Future
